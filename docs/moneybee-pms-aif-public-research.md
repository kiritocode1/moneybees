# Moneybee PMS and AIF public research

Research date: 3 September 2026

## Scope

This note covers the Moneybee investment-management business:

- Queenbee Portfolio Management Services, or PMS
- Flyingbee Investment Fund, a Category III Alternative Investment Fund, or AIF

Broking, depository and investment banking are outside the product analysis. The new website should link to those businesses without mixing their claims, transactions or regulatory details into the PMS and AIF story.

This note separates three types of information:

1. **Public fact** means a statement found on a company or regulator website.
2. **Client-provided claim** means a statement found in the two private Moneybee decks or Shreyam's correspondence. Compliance must approve it before publication.
3. **Recommendation** means a proposed website decision, not a fact about Moneybee.

This is website research, not legal advice. Suprit and Moneybee's legal advisers should approve regulated claims and disclosures.

## Short conclusion

Moneybee's strongest website position is not simply "small-cap investing." Marcellus, SageOne, Abakkus and Alchemy already make versions of that claim.

The stronger position combines four points:

- a PMS record dating to 1 August 2007, once Moneybee approves one consistent performance table
- experience evaluating operating businesses, including management meetings, plant visits and corporate advisory work
- a preference for understandable small and mid-sized Indian businesses with reasonable valuations
- a documented selection process and historical case studies that explain how decisions were made

The website should present Queenbee PMS and Flyingbee AIF as separate products. The long record belongs to the PMS. Flyingbee declared its first close in October 2025 according to the client-provided AIF deck. Presenting the PMS record as an AIF record would be inaccurate.

The public site should explain the approach and establish trust. It should not publish current holdings, active research, buy or sell decisions, private-placement material, internal systems or investor information.

## What PMS and AIF mean

### PMS

SEBI describes PMS as professional management of assets held directly in the investor's name. It distinguishes discretionary, non-discretionary and advisory PMS. SEBI states a minimum investment of Rs 50 lakh. The current Portfolio Managers Regulations also require a portfolio manager to keep its disclosure document on its website and report performance uniformly across disclosures, marketing material, client reports and the website. [SEBI Investor PMS overview](https://investor.sebi.gov.in/pms_final.html) and [SEBI Portfolio Managers Regulations, last amended 10 February 2025](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/regulations/feb-2025/securities-and-exchange-board-of-india-portfolio-managers-regulations-2020-last-amended-on-february-10-2025-_92413.html)

For the website, this means Queenbee needs its own page with:

- direct ownership explained in plain language
- the approved investment approach and investor suitability
- minimum investment
- one consistent, dated performance table
- benchmark and calculation method
- fees and risks
- disclosure document, grievance route and registration details

### AIF

SEBI defines an AIF as a privately pooled investment vehicle. An AIF raises money through private placement and its placement memorandum carries the detailed commercial and risk information. The standard minimum investment is Rs 1 crore. Category III AIFs may use diverse or complex strategies and may use leverage, although the exact strategy depends on the fund documents. [SEBI Alternative Investment Funds Regulations, last amended 19 November 2025](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/regulations/nov-2025/securities-and-exchange-board-of-india-alternative-investment-funds-regulations-2012-last-amended-on-november-19-2025-_97988.html)

For the website, this means Flyingbee needs its own page with:

- a clear statement that it is a Category III AIF
- the approved strategy and investor suitability
- minimum commitment and expected holding period
- its own inception date and performance
- risk, liquidity and redemption language approved against the PPM
- an enquiry or eligibility route, not the full private-placement sales document in public

## Verified public information about Moneybee

### Public company and service claims

Moneybee's current website says it is a SEBI-registered PMS firm and offers non-pooled portfolio management. It explains that clients receive contract emails, monthly portfolio statements and annual capital-gains, expense and income statements. [Moneybee PMS page](https://moneybee.in/pms.php)

The public PMS page names two approaches:

- **Queenbee**, described as a medium to long-term portfolio across small, medium and large-capitalisation companies, with stated single-stock and sector limits of 15% and 30%. The page names BSE 500 as its benchmark.
- **Moneycomb**, described as a long-term approach using undervalued, high-growth, smaller and less-traded stocks. Its active status is unclear from the website.

Moneybee should confirm whether Moneycomb still accepts clients. If it does not, remove it from the new public product structure. [Moneybee PMS page](https://moneybee.in/pms.php)

The public site gives the PMS registration number as **INP000001959**. It also publishes broking and depository registrations, but those belong to other services. Put them behind the group-site switcher and keep them off the PMS and AIF product pages. [Moneybee about page](https://moneybee.in/about.php)

The current public navigation includes PMS, broking, depository, investment advisory and investment banking. It does not give Flyingbee AIF a visible product entry. The new site therefore has to establish the AIF publicly for the first time. [Moneybee homepage](https://moneybee.in/) and [Moneybee PMS page](https://moneybee.in/pms.php)

### Founder and history

The public site describes Dhiren Shah as experienced in equity research and corporate finance. It connects Moneybee's approach with smaller, fast-growing companies and focused management teams. [Moneybee about page](https://moneybee.in/about.php)

The public record is inconsistent on dates:

- the About page says Dhiren Shah formed Moneybee Group in 2007
- the PMS and team timelines say his founder and managing-director role began in 2004
- the client-provided decks also use 2004 and claim more than 45 years of experience

The website should not publish a founding date or a current experience count until Moneybee confirms the wording. A dated career timeline is safer than a number such as "45+ years," which becomes stale every year. [Moneybee about page](https://moneybee.in/about.php), [Moneybee PMS page](https://moneybee.in/pms.php), and [Moneybee team page](https://moneybee.in/team.php)

### Research relationship

The current PMS page says Moneybee uses its sister concern, Moneybee Investment Advisors Private Limited, for bottom-up fundamental research. Shreyam has separately said Moneybee does not hold the SEBI licence required to publish research reports and does not want valuable internal research made public.

These statements are not necessarily incompatible, but the new site needs approved language explaining who performs research and under which regulated entity. It should publish retrospective case studies, not research reports or recommendations. [Moneybee PMS page](https://moneybee.in/pms.php)

### Current public-site problems

The existing public footprint weakens otherwise useful facts:

- The homepage title is only "Money Bee." Its meta description and keywords are empty.
- The site has no public `robots.txt` or `sitemap.xml` at the standard URLs. Both returned 404 during this review.
- The homepage still displays a World Investor Week notice dated November 2020.
- Flyingbee AIF is absent from the visible navigation.
- The About page and founder timeline disagree on 2004 versus 2007.
- Team biographies mix PMS, research, advisory and investment-banking roles.
- Regulatory links occupy the main navigation instead of a structured disclosure centre.
- The PMS page names Moneycomb, which does not appear in the new client materials.

The following material comes from `Moneybee Group Profile - Apr2026 (1).pdf`, `A5.T1.(3).(n) Marketing PPT AIF_14-08-26 (2).pdf`, or Shreyam's messages. The decks restrict public circulation. Treat every item below as awaiting approval.

- Queenbee is the PMS name used in the July 2026 material.
- Flyingbee Investment Fund is a Category III AIF.
- The Flyingbee first close was 30 October 2025.
- The stated AIF minimum is Rs 1 crore.
- The stated AIF portfolio may include listed and pre-IPO unlisted companies, subject to the PPM.
- The AIF registration shown in the deck is IN/AIF3/24-25/1709.

### Process claims

- the investable universe moves from about 6,000 listed companies to about 1,200 within a stated market-cap band
- about 350 companies are shortlisted
- more than 100 are analysed
- about 75 ideas are generated
- about 20 stocks enter the portfolio
- research includes management meetings, plant visits, peer comparison, financial analysis and monitoring

The funnel is useful on a public website because it explains selectivity without revealing the current portfolio. Moneybee should still approve every stage and number. The April deck's portfolio-construction section also refers to 15 to 20 holdings, which should be reconciled with the funnel's approximate 20.

### Historical investment claims

The decks place Fairchem Organics and Privi Speciality Chemicals above 100x, KPI Green Energy and Pitti Engineering above 20x, and Uni Abex Alloy and Tejas Networks above 10x.

These examples need more than company names and multiples. Before publication, obtain:

- purchase and measurement dates
- whether the multiple uses the first purchase, weighted cost or another basis
- treatment of dividends, splits, demergers and partial exits
- whether the position belonged to Queenbee, another PMS approach, a proprietary account or an advisory client
- the illustration and past-performance disclaimer approved by compliance

Until those details exist, the website may say Moneybee has identified historical multibaggers, but it should not lead with the multiples.

### Performance claims requiring reconciliation

The source material contains three different Queenbee or Moneybee PMS return sets:

| Source location | Since inception | Five-year | Recent period shown |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| April group profile, summary page | 22.05% | 33.83% | 91.97% in FY2024 |
| April group profile, table dated 30 April 2026 | 19.20% | 29.05% | -4.90% over one year |

## Competitor set

These firms compete for the same high-net-worth investor's attention. Some overlap directly with Moneybee's small and mid-cap focus. Others set the standard for how an investment manager explains products online. This is a practical website set, not an exhaustive market ranking.

| Firm | Overlap with Moneybee | Useful website lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marcellus | Small and mid-cap discovery PMS | State holdings range, horizon, fees and risk plainly |
| SageOne | Concentrated small-cap PMS plus AIF products | Separate products, suitability, risk and logins |
| Abakkus | Mid and small-cap PMS plus Category III AIF | Give the process a memorable, specific structure |
| Alchemy | Long-running PMS plus Category III AIF and small-cap strategies | Separate legal structure from strategy and compare products clearly |
| WhiteOak | Broader PMS and AIF institutional manager | Keep philosophy, products, performance and disclosures in separate sections |

### Marcellus Investment Managers

#### Why it competes

Little Champs is a small and mid-cap PMS built around finding niche leaders early. Marcellus says it typically targets companies around US$1 billion to US$2 billion in market capitalisation, holds about 30 stocks and recommends a five-to-seven-year horizon. Its stated filters include governance, capital allocation and competitive advantage.

#### What its website does well

The page says who the strategy is for, what it owns in broad terms, how long an investor should stay, and the full fee mechanics. It lists a 1.5% fixed fee, 20% performance fee above a 10% hurdle, high-water-mark treatment, exit loads and the Rs 50 lakh minimum.

#### Implication for Moneybee

"Discovering small companies early" does not distinguish Moneybee by itself. Moneybee needs to add its operating-business experience, historical decisions and downside process.

Source: [Marcellus Little Champs PMS](https://marcellus.in/portfolio-management-services/marcellus-little-champs-pms/) and [Marcellus investment philosophy](https://marcellus.in/investment-philosophy/).

### SageOne Investment Managers

#### Why it competes

SageOne Small Cap Portfolio targets 15 to 20 companies ranked roughly 400th to 1000th by market capitalisation. Its site describes high-growth businesses, 25% or higher compounded earnings growth, management quality and undervaluation.

#### What its website does well

It separates PMS products, domestic AIF, offshore AIF, research, FAQs, disclosures and PMS/AIF logins. The small-cap page states the risk profile and investor suitability, not only the opportunity.

#### Implication for Moneybee

A 15-to-20-stock portfolio, bottom-up research and management quality are category language. Moneybee should show its own process and decisions instead of relying on those phrases.

Source: [SageOne Small Cap Portfolio](https://sageoneinvestments.com/sageone-small-cap-portfolio-ssp/), [investment philosophy](https://sageoneinvestments.com/investment-philosophy/), and [research and insights](https://sageoneinvestments.com/research-insights/).

### Abakkus Asset Manager

#### Why it competes

Abakkus offers both PMS and Category III AIF products. Its Emerging Opportunities PMS and AIF descriptions explicitly favour mid and small-cap companies and use a named 15:15:15 discipline.

#### What its website does well

Products have precise names and short descriptions. The investor area centralises forms, factsheets, presentations and login. Registration information and grievances sit in a dedicated disclosure area. This reduces pressure on the main navigation while keeping regulated material easy to find.

#### Implication for Moneybee

The selection funnel can perform the same role as Abakkus's named framework, but only if Moneybee approves the numbers and explains what each filter means.

Source: [Abakkus products](https://abakkusinvest.com/en/our-products), [investment philosophy](https://abakkusinvest.com/en/about/investment-philosophy), [investor resources](https://abakkusinvest.com/en/investors), and [disclosures](https://abakkusinvest.com/en/disclosures).

### Alchemy Capital Management

#### Why it competes

Alchemy offers PMS and Category III AIF products to high-net-worth individuals, family offices and institutions. Its range includes high-growth, small-cap, micro and small-cap, and entrepreneur-focused strategies. Alchemy markets one PMS strategy with more than 24 years of history.

#### What its website does well

It separates the legal structure from the investment strategy. Product pages repeat direct-onboarding language and registration numbers. The strategy list lets investors compare approaches without reading the corporate history first.

#### Implication for Moneybee

A long record matters, but Moneybee cannot claim category leadership based on age. Alchemy has an older stated PMS track record. Moneybee should present its dated record without "oldest," "unmatched" or similar claims.

Source: [Alchemy PMS](https://www.alchemycapital.com/portfolio-management-services) and [Alchemy AIF](https://www.alchemycapital.com/alternative-investment-funds).

### WhiteOak Capital

#### Why it matters

WhiteOak is a broader institutional benchmark, not Moneybee's closest small-cap peer. It offers PMS and AIF products and explains bottom-up selection, business quality, valuation and portfolio construction in detail.

#### What its website does well

Philosophy, product, performance and portal access have separate destinations. The investment-philosophy page explains business quality and valuation without exposing active research.

#### Implication for Moneybee

The site should separate stable philosophy from changing performance data. This makes compliance review and monthly updates easier.

Source: [WhiteOak investment philosophy](https://www.whiteoakindia.com/investment-approach/investment-philosophy), [PMS products](https://www.whiteoakindia.com/products/PMS/business-leaders), and [AIF product example](https://www.whiteoakindia.com/products/AIF/equity-fund-woisef).

## What is genuinely useful to show

### 1. The PMS history, with one approved table

The July deck's 2007 inception date gives Moneybee a meaningful operating record through multiple market cycles. It should appear as Queenbee PMS performance, never as Flyingbee AIF performance.

Recommended treatment:

- show the as-of date next to the number
- show the benchmark on the same chart
- include the recent negative period
- identify TWRR and whether returns are net of fees and expenses
- link to the disclosure document and full performance table
- update from one approved source, not manually in several sections

### 2. The operating-business lens

Moneybee can explain that its team studies businesses through financial work, management discussions, plant visits and long-term observation. Dhiren Shah's public biography connects equity work with corporate finance, while the private decks add turnaround and advisory experience.

This is more specific than "fundamental research." It also needs careful boundaries. The site should explain experience, not imply privileged access or use investment-banking transactions as PMS performance.

### 3. A clear small and mid-sized business preference

Shreyam's phrase "brick and mortar companies that we understand" is memorable. The polished website version can say:

> We look for understandable Indian businesses with capable management, visible economics and room to grow. We prefer evidence in operations and cash flows over a fashionable market story.

Moneybee should approve this as positioning copy. Avoid using "MSME" as a loose synonym for small-cap. MSME is a legal classification based on investment and turnover, while listed-company market-cap categories use a different basis.

### 4. The selection funnel

The 6,000 to 20 funnel is a good homepage or approach-page graphic. It explains selectivity without publishing an active model or current position.

Public version:

1. listed universe
2. investable size and liquidity screen
3. shortlist
4. company analysis and management work
5. investment candidates
6. portfolio

The public graphic does not need scoring weights, model formulas, watchlists or rejection notes.

### 5. Historical case studies

Three complete case studies would be stronger than a wall of company logos. Each should answer:

- what the company did
- what Moneybee believed the market was missing
- what evidence changed the team's conviction
- what could have invalidated the thesis
- how the business developed
- the dated outcome and required disclaimer

Use investments that compliance approves and that no longer expose a current trading decision.

### 6. Downside discipline

Competitors already speak about growth. Moneybee can give equal weight to rejection and risk:

- governance concerns
- leverage
- valuation without margin of safety
- liquidity
- concentration limits
- thesis changes

Avoid claiming "capital protection" or guaranteed downside control. SEBI rules prohibit guaranteed or assured returns for PMS.

### 7. Compliance as usable information

Compliance should be visible without turning every page into a legal notice. Put the PMS and AIF registration numbers in the footer. Build one disclosure centre for the Investor Charter, disclosure document, grievance process, complaints data, policies and regulator links.

A serious investor should be able to answer four questions quickly:

- Which legal entity manages this product?
- What is the registration number?
- What is the minimum investment?
- Where do I complain or verify the disclosure document?

### 8. Separate routes for investors and distributors

The public site can have two clear enquiry routes. Distributor copy should state the geographies, investor types and onboarding process Moneybee actually supports. Do not say "global distributors" until Moneybee defines eligible jurisdictions and approves the wording.

## Claims that sound distinctive but are not

The following claims appear across competitor sites and should not lead Moneybee's homepage:

- bottom-up research
- high-conviction portfolio
- long-term wealth creation
- strong management
- reasonable valuations
- sector agnostic
- benchmark outperformance
- disciplined risk management
- hidden gems
- client first

These ideas can stay as supporting language. The evidence has to carry the page: dates, process, case studies, risk rules and accountable people.

## What belongs in public, gated and private areas

### Public website

Publish after approval:

- company and team history
- Queenbee PMS and Flyingbee AIF as separate products
- approved investment philosophy
- eligibility and minimum investment
- approved fees or fee ranges with an illustration
- dated performance tables and benchmarks
- historical case studies
- selection funnel
- monthly PMS factsheets if approved for public distribution
- regulatory registrations and documents
- grievance and complaint information
- careers and distributor enquiries
- contact details and client-login handoff

### Gated or supplied after qualification

Keep behind an eligibility check, login or direct request where appropriate:

- AIF placement memorandum and subscription material
- detailed AIF terms from the PPM
- client agreements and onboarding documents
- investor-specific reports and statements
- distributor commercial terms
- private factsheets or presentations whose distribution is restricted

### Keep private

Do not publish:

- current buy and sell decisions
- active watchlists or research reports
- model weights, scoring formulas or unpublished valuation work
- investor names, holdings, returns or contact data
- API documentation, AWS architecture, credentials or security configuration
- CRM data and applicant ratings
- management-meeting notes
- confidential deck files
- claims based on investment-banking transactions presented as PMS or AIF results

## Information that may be too much even when it is technically public

The current website puts many policies, forms, circulars and regulator links in the primary navigation. That makes the business hard to understand. Keep the documents available, but move them into a searchable disclosure centre.

Avoid these public-page patterns:

- a homepage dominated by legal text
- a full team directory with personal or irrelevant biography details
- every historical deal completed by another Moneybee business
- every return period repeated in several places
- a long list of multibagger names without dates and method
- a live portfolio holdings list
- a detailed explanation of AWS, CRM and WhatsApp plumbing
- a careers page that exposes internal ratings or interview logic
- a generic content library created only to target search keywords

The test is simple. Public information should help an investor understand the firm, compare the products, judge the method, verify regulation or make contact. If it does none of those, remove it from the main site or place it in the disclosure centre.

## Recommended website message order

### Homepage

1. Moneybee manages concentrated Indian equity portfolios through PMS and AIF structures.
2. State the small and mid-sized business focus in plain language.
3. Show one approved Queenbee PMS record with its benchmark and date.
4. Explain the selection funnel.
5. Link to three approved historical case studies.
6. Introduce the people responsible for investing and compliance.
7. Let the visitor choose PMS, AIF, distributor or careers.
8. End with registrations, risks and the disclosure centre.

### Queenbee PMS page

- direct ownership and Rs 50 lakh minimum
- active status and exact investment approach
- approved benchmark, inception date and performance
- portfolio construction and risk controls
- fees with hurdle and high-water-mark illustration
- reporting and service model
- suitability, risks and enquiry
- disclosure document

### Flyingbee AIF page

- Category III structure and Rs 1 crore minimum
- first close and current status
- listed versus unlisted mandate exactly as stated in the PPM
- time horizon, liquidity and redemption terms
- its own short performance history
- sponsor or manager details
- approved risk language
- eligibility-led enquiry and gated documents

### Investment approach page

- business characteristics Moneybee seeks
- what Moneybee rejects
- screening funnel
- management and plant-level work
- valuation and downside rules
- monitoring and sell discipline at a high level
- historical case studies

### Compliance centre

- registration information by product and legal entity
- PMS disclosure document
- Investor Charter
- grievance contacts and SCORES
- complaint disclosures
- policies and risk notices
- privacy and data handling
- direct-onboarding language if required

## Decisions needed from Moneybee

Before final copy or performance design, obtain written answers to these questions:

1. Is the firm history 2004 or 2007? What exact sentence should the site use?
2. Is Moneycomb active? If so, does it belong on this website beside Queenbee?
3. Which legal entity is the portfolio manager for Queenbee?
4. Which legal entities are the Flyingbee fund, sponsor and manager?
5. Which PMS performance table is approved for publication?
6. Which AIF performance table is approved for publication?
7. Can the public site reproduce the screening funnel?
8. Which historical investments may become public case studies?
9. What dates and calculation method support each multibagger multiple?
10. How should the website describe the research relationship with Moneybee Investment Advisors?
11. Which factsheets are public, and which require qualification or login?
12. Can the site publicly state listed and unlisted allocation ranges for Flyingbee, or must those remain in the PPM?
13. Which fees or fee ranges may appear publicly for each product?
14. Which jurisdictions can Moneybee and its distributors actively approach?
15. Who gives final approval for product copy, performance, case studies and disclaimers?
