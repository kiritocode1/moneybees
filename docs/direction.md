> client's requirements in [`client-brief.md`](./client-brief.md).
>
> The client brief tells us **what must exist**. This document tells us **how it should feel and

---

## The one line

> **The site should make a wealthy investor feel this firm has already managed capital responsibly
> for decades — even if they are hearing about it for the first time.**

Every type choice, every line of copy, every animation either reinforces that or works against it.
There is no neutral.

---

## Who we are writing for

An HNI with ₹50 lakh to ₹20 crore to place is **not buying returns. They are buying confidence.**

By the time they reach us they have already heard every pitch — *market-beating returns*, *expert
fund managers*, *AI-powered investing*, *long-term wealth creation*. Those phrases are noise. The
real question behind every page they read is:

> *"Can I trust these people with a meaningful portion of my family's wealth?"*

### What actually moves them

| They care about | Not about |
| --- | --- |
| **How much can I lose?** — downside protection, drawdown management, risk-adjusted returns | Highest CAGR |
| **Process** — a disciplined framework that survives cycles | Predictions about where the market is going |
| **Transparency** — why each holding exists, monthly commentary, clear fees | A perfect year |
| **Alignment** — managers with their own money in, no churn | Sales enthusiasm |
| **Consistency** — compounding through multiple environments | "We did 45% last year" |
| **A reason to exist** — a sharp, specific philosophy | A list of services |
| **Access** — a real line to the people making decisions | A support ticket |
| **After-tax reality** — turnover, LTCG, succession | Gross headline numbers |
| **Visible compliance** — SEBI, custodian, auditor, disciplinary history | Hidden legal pages |
| **Simplicity** — plain language | Proprietary-framework jargon |

### Why clients leave

Almost never bad returns. It's poor communication, hidden risk, strategy drift, overpromising, and
an inability to explain a decision. **Design the site so none of those are ever true of us.**

## The feeling

Open the site. There should be:

- No bright gradient.
- No floating glassmorphic cards.
- No bull, no candlestick chart, no upward arrow.
- No animation competing for attention.

Just a calm, confident, restrained interface. Generous whitespace. Large type. Very little movement.

**The site should almost whisper.** It does not try to convince you. It assumes you are intelligent.

The register we want is **"modern but rooted in tradition"** — the client's own phrase, and it's the
right one. Not a century-old Swiss private bank (too sleepy, and untrue to Moneybee). Not a fintech
dashboard (fatal). Somewhere closer to **a sharp, modern investment house that has been doing this
long enough to have stopped shouting.**

Reference the *restraint* of Apple, Aesop, and Porsche; the *editorial confidence* of a good annual
report; the *information density done gracefully* of Goldman Sachs. Reference almost nothing from
Indian financial-services web design, which is where every competitor already is.

> **The paradox to hold onto:** the more money someone is considering entrusting to you, the less
> impressed they are by *more*. A site comfortable saying less — but saying it exceptionally well —
> leaves the stronger impression.

## Voice

### Principles

**1. Conviction over enthusiasm.**
State what we believe. Do not sell it.

> ❌ We provide innovative, tailored financial solutions designed to maximise your wealth.
> ✅ Markets reward patience more often than prediction.

**2. Concrete over abstract.**
Every claim should be checkable or it should be cut.

> ❌ Personalised service and dedicated support.
> ✅ A named relationship manager, a quarterly portfolio review, and direct access to the investment team.

**3. Plain over technical.**
Jargon signals insecurity to this audience.

> ❌ Proprietary AI-driven alpha-generation framework.
> ✅ We buy outstanding businesses at sensible prices and hold them for years.

**4. Own the risk.**
Most firms hide this section. We elevate it. Confidence grows when a firm discusses risk willingly.

> ✅ Every investment carries risk. Managing it is our responsibility, and here is how we do it.

**5. Never create urgency.**
No countdowns, no "limited allocation", no scarcity framing. It reads as desperate to the exact
person we are trying to reach.

### Banned outright

🚫 Guaranteed returns · 🚫 Highest returns in India · 🚫 Double your wealth · 🚫 Beat the market every
year · 🚫 AI picks stocks better than humans · 🚫 Limited-time opportunity · 🚫 Don't miss out ·
🚫 Invest Now (as a primary CTA) · 🚫 Why Choose Us · 🚫 Unlock / Supercharge / Revolutionise

### Naming things

Words carry the whole positioning. Use the right column.

| Conventional | Ours |
| --- | --- |
| Blog | **From the investment desk** |
| Why Choose Us | **Why families entrust us with their wealth** |
| Invest Now | **Schedule a conversation** |
| Our Services | **What we do** / split by audience |
| Contact Us | **Speak with our team** |
| Testimonials | *(cut — replace with investment letters)* |
| Ready to get started? | **Every meaningful investment relationship begins with a conversation.** |
| Learn More | *(name the destination: "Read our investment approach")* |

### The closing note, everywhere

End pages the way a partner would end a meeting — not the way a funnel would.

> Every meaningful investment relationship begins with a conversation.
> We'd like to understand your objectives before discussing ours.

## The narrative

Moneybee's actual differentiator, from its existing positioning: **fundamental, bottom-up research
into overlooked businesses**, with real strength in small and ultra-small caps, and decades of
capital-markets experience behind it.

That is specific, defensible, and rare. It should be the spine of the site.

### Candidate taglines

- **"Small steps, Big Outcomes."** — the client's. Strong: it's on-brand for a *bee*, and it's
  literally what compounding is. Recommended as the primary.
- **"Finding value where the market is not looking."** — sharper on the investment differentiator.
  Works well as the hero sub-line beneath the tagline, or as the philosophy-section headline.

### Four principles to carry through the site

1. **Fundamental research** — understand the business before valuing the security.
2. **Independent thinking** — conviction comes from evidence, not consensus.
3. **Long-term ownership** — let business performance, not market noise, drive outcomes.
4. **Valuation discipline** — a good company is not a good investment at every price.

## Visual language

### Colour

**Black, white/ivory, and yellow.** This is the client's stated palette and it is correct — Moneybee
is a *bee*. Lean into it rather than defaulting to the navy-and-gold every Indian PMS site uses.

- Black and near-black for type and structure.
- Warm off-white / ivory as the ground, not pure `#FFF` — pure white reads cheap at this scale.
- Yellow strictly as an **accent**: key phrases, active states, one CTA per view. Never as a
  background wash, never as a gradient.
- The restraint *is* the luxury signal. If yellow appears more than a few times per screen, it stops
  reading as accent and starts reading as a startup.

> ⚠️ The client brief contradicts itself here — the Theme section says black/white/yellow, the
> homepage description says "white/grey with navy blue highlights". We follow **black/white/yellow**
> as the brand palette. Confirm with the client.

### Typography

One modern sans-serif, used at genuine scale. Large, confident headlines carrying the layout — type
*is* the primary design element. Long-form sections (philosophy, letters, bios) get real measure and
leading; treat them as editorial, not as marketing copy blocks.

### Layout

The client wants an information-rich, comprehensive grid — reasonable for a firm with AIF, PMS,
advisory, and IB. Deliver density **without** clutter by giving each section a clear rank. The failure
mode of the reference sites is that every section shouts equally, so nothing reads as important.

**Hierarchy, most to least prominent:** philosophy → strategies/performance → team → insights →
everything else.

### Imagery

Original photography wherever possible — the office, the team, actual research materials. The
client's Design section says avoid stock overload, and that instinct should win over the later
Imagery note that permits a stock mix.

Where we must use stock, avoid: handshakes, skyscraper glass, rising arrows, generic "diverse team in
meeting room". Prefer quiet, textural, editorial images. Team portraits should be consistent —
one shoot, one setup, one treatment.

### Motion

Subtle hover states on buttons and cards, small loading state on the primary CTA. **No full-page
transitions** (the client is right — they cost LCP and read as decoration).

Motion budget: if a visitor *notices* an animation, it's too much.

## Reconciliations

Where our direction meets the client brief, these are the calls.

| Tension | Call |
| --- | --- |
| Brief wants a **Quick Stats bar** (AUM, years, PAN India presence). Our instinct was to cut stat blocks. | **Keep them.** The earlier objection was to *empty* stats ("24/7", "100%"). AUM, years in market, and families served are genuine institutional trust metrics for a PMS/AIF, and SEBI-adjacent audiences expect them. Present them as facts in a quiet row — not as animated counters, not as hero decoration. |
| Brief puts **Performance high in the nav**. Our direction said don't lead with returns. | **Both.** Prominent *placement*, restrained *presentation*. Performance is the second or third nav item; the page itself opens with "Returns matter. The path taken to achieve them matters too," then gives benchmark comparison, rolling returns, drawdowns, and TWRR methodology. Analytical, never celebratory. No giant "42%" card. |
| Brief allows a **stock photo mix**; its own Design section says avoid stock overload. | Original photography first. Stock only as a fallback, and only textural/editorial. |
| Brief's theme says **black/white/yellow**; its homepage description says **navy highlights**. | Black/white/yellow. Flag to client. |
| Brief recommends **WordPress**. | **Next.js on Vercel.** See below. |
| Brief wants a **sticky "Invest Now"** CTA. | Sticky CTA yes — but worded **"Schedule a conversation"**. "Invest Now" is the wrong verb for a ₹50L+ decision that legally requires onboarding and suitability checks. |
| Brief wants **testimonials** as trust signals. | Replace with investment letters, sector notes, and CIO commentary. For this audience, demonstrated thinking outperforms quoted praise — and testimonials in Indian financial marketing carry compliance risk. |
| Brief lists an **AI chat box**. | Deprioritise to post-launch, and only if it routes to a human. An LLM chat widget on a SEBI-regulated site is a compliance surface, not a feature. |

### Answering the platform footnote

The client's comparison chart concluded WordPress because it assumed *coding* means high in-house
maintenance, manual security patching, manual backups, and a developer needed for every change. On
**Next.js + Vercel** that mapping no longer holds:

| Their concern | On Next.js + Vercel |
| --- | --- |
| Security updates are manual | No plugin surface to patch — the largest WordPress attack vector doesn't exist |
| Backups need automating | Git is the backup; every deploy is an immutable, instantly revertible version |
| DDoS / WAF must be added | Included at the edge (Vercel Firewall / Cloudflare in front) |
| Speed is "variable" | Static-first rendering hits the 90+ PageSpeed and <2.5s LCP targets far more reliably than WordPress + plugins |
| Developer needed for every change | Only if we build it that way — pair with a headless CMS so the Moneybee team edits content, insights, and performance data without us |
| Host access / handover | Full repo, domain, and hosting ownership transfers to Moneybee; same handover doc, same training session |

**The thing we must actually solve** is the client's real underlying requirement: *moderate in-house
maintenance*. That means a CMS layer for Insights, team bios, and performance figures is
**non-negotiable scope**, not a nice-to-have. Without it their WordPress instinct is correct.

## Structural direction

Not a final sitemap — the shape the pages should take.

1. **Hero** — tagline + one-sentence articulation of the approach. One primary CTA
   ("Schedule a conversation"). A restrained service line beneath: PMS · AIF · Investment Advisory ·
   Investment Banking · Broking & Depository.
2. **Institutional trust strip** — SEBI registration and category, NSE/BSE membership, CDSL, years in
   capital markets, AUM. Currently buried at the bottom of moneybee.in; it belongs near the top.
3. **Philosophy** — the four principles. The intellectual centre of the site.
4. **Split the audiences** — investors (PMS, AIF, advisory, broking, login) vs. businesses and
   promoters (fundraising, M&A, strategic advisory). The current site mixes them, so a visitor cannot
   tell what Moneybee primarily *is*.
5. **Strategies & Performance** — per-product cards, returns vs. benchmark, risk-adjusted ratios,
   fee structure with the illustrative ₹50 lakh example, downloadable factsheet/PPM.
6. **Sector knowledge** — short essays on the sectors Moneybee actually researches, instead of a logo
   wall.
7. **Team** — CIO/founder first and larger, then investment, research, dealing, operations,
   compliance. Answers key-man risk without being asked.
8. **From the investment desk** — quarterly letters, PM commentary, sector notes.
9. **What investors can expect from us** — makes "personalised service" concrete.
10. **Investor & Regulatory Centre** — the full SEBI wall lives here, linked from a concise footer
    summary on every page. Accessible, not cluttering.
11. **Careers**, **Login**, **Contact**.

## Open questions

Settle these before layout work — each changes the build materially.

1. **One site or two?** The Linear brief says separate PMS/AIF from `moneybeesecurities.in`. These
   client documents are titled *"Moneybee Advisors & Securities"* and scoped to *Moneybee Securities
   Pvt Ltd*, describing one combined site. A standalone PMS/AIF site and a single group site with
   split audience paths are different products.
2. **Palette:** black/white/yellow or navy? The brief says both.
3. **Tagline:** "Small steps, Big Outcomes" confirmed as primary?
4. **CMS choice** — required for the in-house maintenance commitment above.
5. **Investor login** — build or embed? SEBI-mandated 2FA/OTP and portfolio data make this the single
   largest scope item, and it may belong to an existing portal vendor rather than to us.
6. **Performance data** — who supplies it, in what format, at what frequency, and who signs it off?
   The brief requires compliance sign-off on all public-facing data.
7. Contact Us page and Newsletter — marked **pending** in the client's own notes.
