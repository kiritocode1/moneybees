## The correction that matters most

The meeting brief gets Shreyam's return point wrong.

It currently treats the claim as uncertain on four questions. The two decks answer three of them directly:

- The long record belongs to **Moneybee PMS, named Queenbee in the latest table**. It does not belong to Flyingbee AIF.
- The stated benchmark is **S&P BSE 500 TRI**.
- The latest stated since-inception return is **19.44% CAGR for Queenbee against 9.90% for the benchmark**, as at 31 July 2026.

The difference is **9.54 percentage points of annualised return**, not "more than 7%" if we use the latest deck.

The same page says Rs. 1 million invested on 1 August 2007 would have become:

- **Rs. 28.85 million with Moneybee PMS**
- **Rs. 5.97 million with S&P BSE 500 TRI**
- **Rs. 22.88 million more with Moneybee PMS**, equal to Rs. 2.288 crore
- **4.83 times the benchmark's ending value**

Those last two figures are simple arithmetic from the values in the deck. Moneybee did not print them as separate claims.

The period is also wrong in the meeting brief. From 1 August 2007 to 31 July 2026 is effectively **19 years**, not 17 years.

Source: `A5.T1.(3).(n) Marketing PPT AIF_14-08-26 (2).pdf`, page 12.

### Safe working version of points 1 to 4

> Queenbee PMS reports a since-inception CAGR of 19.44% from 1 August 2007 to 31 July 2026, compared with 9.90% for the S&P BSE 500 TRI. The deck states that Rs. 10 lakh invested at inception would have grown to Rs. 2.885 crore, compared with Rs. 59.7 lakh in the benchmark.

This is the useful claim. It must be shown as dated PMS performance with the benchmark, methodology note and full disclaimer. It should never be described as the AIF's 17-year return.

## How Moneybee says it manages risk

The documents describe a practical framework, though some limits remain unspecified.

### 1. Valuation risk

Moneybee says it avoids buying without an adequate margin of safety. It evaluates value against price and expects the return to justify the risk.

Sources: AIF marketing deck, pages 4 and 7; group profile, pages 12 and 16.

### 2. Market risk

The stated response to adverse economic, industry or market conditions is patience rather than short-term trading. The PMS material pairs this with a minimum three-year investment horizon or holding while the thesis remains intact.

Sources: AIF marketing deck, page 7; group profile, pages 16 and 17.

### 3. Liquidity risk

For listed securities, Moneybee says it looks for enough trading volume to enter and exit without materially moving the price. The PMS deck also says it maintains a reasonable cash allocation for liquidity and volatility.

For unlisted AIF positions, the deck names IPOs, buybacks and strategic sales as possible exits and says the fund should define the exit route before investing.

Sources: AIF marketing deck, page 7; group profile, page 16.

### 4. Concentration risk

The PMS portfolio is described as **15 to 20 small and mid-cap stocks**, with a **maximum sector allocation of 30%**. Both decks say a maximum single-stock exposure will be set, but neither presentation gives the number.

Sources: group profile, pages 16 and 17; AIF marketing deck, page 7.

### 5. Business and governance filters

Moneybee lists these warning signs:

- poor corporate governance
- low promoter holding
- expensive valuation
- very high leverage
- high institutional ownership
- fashionable or "hot" sectors

It says it does not trade for the short term, chase hot stocks, make impulsive decisions, or follow news and market commentary as an investment process. The AIF material also says it does not use derivatives and F&O. Compliance should check that last statement against the PPM before it appears as a firm fund restriction.

Source: AIF marketing deck, page 6. The PMS list on group profile page 15 is similar but not identical.

### 6. Monitoring and exits

The AIF process includes news monitoring, quarterly reviews, discussions with management and intermediaries, and watching sector trends. It names two exit triggers:

- achievement of the target price
- deviation from the expected growth strategy

The PMS deck also says it exits after the investment objective is achieved and looks for changes that could break the thesis.

Sources: AIF marketing deck, page 5; group profile, pages 13 and 17.

## How the investment process works

### PMS selection funnel

The April group profile gives the clearest numbers:

1. Start with roughly **6,000 companies**.
2. Roughly **1,200 companies** fall within the stated Rs. 150 crore to Rs. 2,000 crore market-cap range and are considered investable.
3. Roughly **350 companies** are shortlisted.
4. More than **100 companies** receive macro and company-level analysis.
5. Roughly **75 stocks** reach idea generation.
6. Roughly **20 stocks** enter portfolio construction and risk monitoring.

The work listed across those stages includes annual reports, internal screeners, management quality, fundamentals, management meetings, plant visits, peer comparison, financial models, liquidity, sector exposure, risk-reward analysis, quarterly reviews and sell discipline.

Source: group profile, page 13.

This is the actual diagram the website can use. The meeting brief calls it "the AIF pyramid on page 11." That is inaccurate:

- AIF marketing deck page 11 is the multibagger list.
- Group profile page 11 is a "Why Moneybee?" summary and mentions a pyramiding approach to position building.
- The numbered selection funnel is on group profile page 13.
- The AIF's six-stage selection process is on AIF marketing deck page 5.

### AIF selection process

The AIF deck gives six stages without funnel counts:

1. screen using software, proposals, intermediaries, annual reports and news
2. shortlist for management credibility, fundamentals, competitive advantage, macro trends and sector tailwinds
3. perform due diligence through management meetings, plant visits, peer comparison, research and financial analysis
4. decide using liquidity, sector exposure, risk-reward and valuation
5. monitor news, quarterly results, management and trends
6. exit at target price or when the expected growth strategy changes

Source: AIF marketing deck, page 5.

## Shreyam's point 5, what the decks do and do not support

The exact statement about India growing 7% to 8% for 20 years and micro, small and medium companies growing 30% to 40% year over year does **not** appear in either presentation.

The April group profile does support the broad thesis. It says:

- Moneybee focuses on small and medium-sized companies that lead their niches.
- India's growth creates opportunities for smaller enterprises.
- Moneybee expects smaller businesses to grow faster than GDP.
- Early investment can capture business growth and valuation rerating.
- Moneybee has worked with smaller businesses through advisory and investing for about two decades.

Source: group profile, pages 4 and 14.

The April deck also claims that the Nifty Small Cap Index returned 18.8% CAGR over ten years against 12.5% for the Nifty 50, and that small caps accounted for 86% of multibaggers above 3x. The deck gives no source or measurement date for either claim. They are not ready for a public website.

The following parts of Shreyam's wording need a source or written compliance approval:

- India growing 7% to 8% for the next 20 years
- target companies growing 30% to 40% year over year
- use of "micro" and "MSME"
- "brick and mortar" as a formal strategy restriction

Small-cap, mid-cap and MSME are not interchangeable terms. Market capitalisation defines the first two. Investment and turnover thresholds define MSME status. The website should not call the portfolio an MSME strategy unless that is factually true for the investee companies and Moneybee approves the classification.

### Safe working version of point 5

> Moneybee looks for smaller Indian companies with capable management, sound economics and room to grow faster than the wider economy. The team studies financials and operations, meets management, visits plants where relevant, and stays invested while the original thesis remains intact.

This wording follows the documents without adding the unsupported 7% to 8% and 30% to 40% forecasts.
