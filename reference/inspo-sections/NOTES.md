## 3. Recurring layout patterns (observed)

1. **Active-row list.** A long list in ~28-48px type where one row is full black (or white on dark)
   and the rest sit at ~30% opacity; scroll or hover moves the active row and swaps one image. Seen in
   Telha (team names), AUAR (values, team table), Kaleida (cities), Kononenko (numbers), K95 (first
   names).
2. **Label / number / body row.** Hairline rule, then a small number ("01"), a small caps label, and
   one or two body columns on the right. DADO ("01 Company origins"), Lama Lama process, Aspen
   services, Telha chapters.
3. **Figure + caption stack.** Large figure (~64-96px) with a two-line caption set smaller and
   indented, stacked vertically, not in a 4-up row. Kononenko, AUAR, Aspen.
4. **Plain tables for credentials.** Awards and press as Year / Project / Award columns (Telha),
   people as Name / Position / Location (Revelatio, AUAR).
5. **Eyebrow + statement.** Tiny uppercase or bracketed label above a 32-40px sentence. "(About)",
   "OUR VALUES", "[ WE ARE LAMA LAMA ]", "WHO YOU'LL ACTUALLY WORK WITH".
6. **Pull quote as a divider** between intro and team (Telha), full width, ~40px.
7. **Carousel with counter.** "01 / 20" plus PREVIOUS / NEXT, one person in colour and the others
   muted (United Carriers, Aspen).
8. **Footer as three bands.** Closing statement + nav + offices/clocks, then legal, then a
   full-bleed wordmark (Aspen, Noho, RIPE).
9. **Persistent small CTA.** A pill fixed on screen ("Let's work together") [Telha], or "Contact" as
   the only filled button in the header [Aspen].

Motion seen: scroll-driven opacity on list rows, odometer digits, pixel/dot-matrix dissolve on
portraits, stacking pinned rows, cards fanning out (LxL), photos on an orbit (Kononenko), a paper
letter unfolding (Driftime).

## 4. Fit for a restrained, SEBI-regulated investment firm [inf]

**Fits**
- Aspen's whole register. A partner-run specialist firm addressing sophisticated clients; the copy is
  specific ("the person who will run your search from intake to offer") and the only boast is tenure.
  Moneybee analogues: "Who you'll actually deal with", "Run by the people who make the calls",
  "Talk to a partner", "Start a conversation".
- Plain declarative about H1 (Telha, AUAR): "Moneybee is a Mumbai-based … firm." reads as an
  institution, which matches the site direction in memory.
- Numbered chapters with small caps labels (Telha, DADO, Lama Lama structure without its tone). They
  give the page an order you can cite in a deck and keep the page calm.
- Short value imperatives (AUAR) if the words are plain and checkable, e.g. "Own the research",
  "Buy what we understand". Two-word paired nouns (Kononenko "Precision in Development") are also
  safe but go generic quickly.
- Figure + caption stack for facts that are not performance: year founded, years of research, number
  of companies covered, AUM only if compliance allows. Kononenko's "2011 Year of Foundation" is the
  model: a fact with a label.
- Tables for credentials (Telha awards/press) and for team (Revelatio/AUAR Name / Role). A table
  looks like a register, which is what a regulated firm should look like.
- "The people behind …" (AUAR, United Carriers). "The people behind the portfolio" or "The people
  behind the research" works. United Carriers' two-tone grey/black two-line headline also suits the
  light-sans editorial style.
- Active-row list for team or philosophy, since it is motion driven by the reader and uses only
  opacity. Fits one-accent editorial design.
- Aspen-style footer: a closing line, nav, office and a live Mumbai clock, then legal, then a
  wordmark band. The legal band is where SEBI registration numbers and disclaimers go.
- Pull quote from a founder, but only about method, not results.

**Too playful or risky**
- Self-descriptions as characters: "DIGITAL FREAKS", "CULTURE CAUGHT ON CAMERA", "Our team is our
  business", Brand Appart's "Lootbox" drawers, LxL's tilted pastel cards. They read as agency
  recruitment, not stewardship of money.
- "Favorite Team" (Kononenko): cute, vague.
- "The World's Most Ambitious Brands Choose to Work With Us": superlative client claim. For an
  investment firm this sits close to promotional language regulators dislike.
- "Be bold, be radical" and "WE OWN THE OUTCOME": bold outcome language is a problem when the outcome
  is investment return.
- Testimonial carousels (Aspen). Under SEBI's advertisement rules for regulated intermediaries,
  client testimonials and implied return promises are restricted; check with the client's compliance
  officer before using any quote block. [inf, verify]
- Odometer figures, orbiting photos, chromatic-offset type, sticker mascots, recolouring wordmarks:
  motion that performs. Keep figures static or set once on entry.
- Performance numbers styled as "proof". Past returns need the mandated disclosures next to them;
  the "numbers" section should carry facts about the firm, not returns, unless compliance shapes it.
