## The important distinction

Moneybee already has a specific line motif: straight vertical stripes with irregular widths and spacing. Further Forecast contributes a different language of thin, curved conceptual diagrams. The two can coexist, but they have different sources and jobs.

The stripes establish Moneybee identity. The curves explain research, selection, relationships, and other approved concepts. Do not describe the Further Forecast orbits as Moneybee's original brand motif.

## Stripe construction

The stripes have flat colour, square ends, and no rounded corners. Widths vary from hairlines to broad bars. Gaps vary too. There is no evident regular repeating unit in the supplied strip, and it should not be replaced by an evenly spaced repeating gradient.

The strip has no chart labels, axes, or measured values. It is not a barcode to decode, a stock chart, or a performance history. Its geometry must remain separate from financial data.

If we recreate it, preserve the stripe order and relative widths from `29.jpg` and compare the output against that image. The JPG introduces compression and antialiasing, so sampled edge pixels are not additional brand colours. Exact production colour values should come from supplied vector artwork or brand specifications.

## Typography and illustration

The serif belongs to the logo. The printed layouts use sans-serif text, including bold condensed-looking uppercase headings. This does not establish that the new website should use that heading style. The selected Further Forecast references use quieter, mostly regular-weight type, and the current brief asks for a clean and serious PMS/AIF presentation.

The bee supplies personality within otherwise simple compositions. Some print spreads use visual jokes and financial wordplay. We should preserve the identity without importing that copy or tone into performance disclosures, product explanations, or the hero.

The case study's inclusion of a research-report template is historical brand evidence. It does not override Shreyam's instruction against publishing investment research on the new site.

## How this changes our direction

Use the supplied logo unchanged, white/grey page foundations, black text, and orange accents. Keep the original stripes available for a restrained boundary, document treatment, or chapter transition. Use the Further Forecast curve compositions within educational visuals, with Moneybee content and palette.

Do not place a dense stripe field behind a thin-line diagram. Both need visual space and would compete. A page can establish the brand through the logo and one stripe treatment, then let the educational drawing use its own clear area.

The selected Moneybee research-selection image remains a separate composition target. Sixofus adds evidence for the surrounding identity; it does not justify changing that selected drawing's geometry.

No stripe animation is established by these assets. The page's carousel behaviour belongs to the agency portfolio viewer, not to the Moneybee identity. Static stripes are the appropriate baseline. Any later motion needs a specific purpose and separate review.

## Scope boundary

This reference comes from Moneybee Securities. Retain the identity relationship, but keep the new PMS/AIF product copy, performance, and user journeys separate from broking and investment banking. The current Linear requirements remain authoritative for product scope.
