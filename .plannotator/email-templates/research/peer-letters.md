## Cross-source findings

Observed:
- Almost none of them use a salutation. Marcellus, Unifi and Buoyant have none. Carnelian uses "Greetings from Team Carnelian!". Abakkus MF uses "Dear Partners and Investors."
- Sign-off is a team, not a person: "Regards, Team Marcellus". Individuals appear as the named PM per strategy (Marcellus) or as the CIO essay author.
- Performance in text: a single sentence with the period, the number and the benchmark, then a table. Buoyant is clearest: states "post fees and expenses", "in compliance with SEBI regulations", includes since-inception, 5y, 3y, 2y, 1y, 6m, 3m, 1m, and annualisation footnote. Unifi adds pre-tax and post-tax NAV, and "post-expense, pre-carry, pre-tax" for the CRISIL AIF comparison, with peer count.
- Pattern for stock-level content: the thesis, the quarter's numbers, "we remain constructive", then "Key risks" (Unifi). Marcellus lists portfolio changes with an addition rationale.
- Length: email-style updates run 1,700 to 3,000 words; factsheets 10 to 13 pages. All are long. None is short enough to be a pure email body; the email is a cover to a PDF or web page (inferred).
- Disclaimers are long (550 words at Marcellus) and sit at the end. Verbatim "not verified by SEBI" appears in Marcellus and Buoyant.
- Confidentiality footer on every page for AIF (Unifi: "Private and Confidential. Circulation to Unit Holders Only").
- Closing statement of next communication (Unifi: "We will be writing to you again after the 3rd quarter results.") and invitation to write to the relationship manager.

Inferred:
- A boutique with a 15 to 20 stock book can follow Unifi's holdings-with-rationale pattern at much shorter length.
- Because the peers do not use "Dear X", a plain "Dear Investor" or "Dear Client" is not required by industry habit; it is a choice. The firm voice ("we", "Team") is the norm.

## Recommended section orders (for Moneybee)

All inferred from the above, not copied from any one source.

(a) Monthly PMS client update email (short cover; full factsheet attached or linked)
1. Subject: "QueenBee PMS: portfolio update, [Month Year]"
2. Salutation: "Dear [Name]," (the firm's choice; peers omit it)
3. One-sentence purpose: what is attached and the period covered
4. Performance: one sentence (period, portfolio return, benchmark return) then a small table (1m, 3m, 6m, 1y, 3y, since inception; benchmark; "net of fees and expenses"; annualised above one year; source). Report shortfalls at the same prominence.
5. What changed: additions, exits and weight changes with one-line reason each (Marcellus pattern)
6. Top holdings and sector split (or pointer to factsheet)
7. Short outlook paragraph, 3 to 5 sentences, no forecasts of returns
8. Next communication date; contact for questions
9. Sign-off "Regards," + firm name + named relationship contact
10. Disclaimer block and SEBI registration number (wording owned by the SEBI worker)

(b) Quarterly AIF investor update email (Flyingbee)
1. Subject: "Flyingbee Investment Fund: quarterly update, [quarter]" with "Private and confidential" line
2. Salutation, one-line purpose
3. Fund facts line: scheme, corpus/units outstanding, manager commitment (Unifi shows manager's own investment)
4. Fund commentary: 1 to 2 paragraphs on the quarter and portfolio actions
5. Performance: NAV/return per unit class, pre-tax vs post-tax if relevant, benchmark; state basis (post-expense, pre-carry, pre-tax is how Unifi words it); first close Oct 2025 means short history, show since-inception only and say so rather than annualising
6. Top holdings with rationale and "Key risks" line each (Unifi), or pointer to attached report
7. Risk and liquidity: concentration, market-cap split, liquidity
8. Next report date, invitation to write to relationship manager
9. Sign-off, confidentiality footer ("Circulation to unit holders only")
10. Disclaimer and AIF registration

(c) First introduction email to a prospect
Gap: none of the peers publish one. Inferred order: subject naming the firm and reason for writing > salutation > who we are in one sentence (SEBI-registered portfolio manager since 2007, QueenBee approach, 15 to 20 holdings) > why writing to this person (referral or context) > what we propose (a call or the factsheet) > attached/linked factsheet with period of returns net of fees > one clear next step > sign-off with named person and SEBI registration > short disclaimer ("not an offer or solicitation", "past performance is not indicative"). Buoyant's disclaimer says the document is on a private placement basis and the recipient is not a client by virtue of receiving it; reuse that idea.

(d) Meeting follow-up email
Gap: none published. Inferred order: subject "Following our meeting on [date]" > thank you in one line > two or three points discussed, restated neutrally > what is attached (factsheet, disclosure document, account-opening steps if asked) > what we have agreed to do and by when > what we ask of them > sign-off. No performance claims in the body; keep them in the attachment.

(e) Event invitation
Gap: Carnelian publishes recordings of quarterly calls but no invitation text. Inferred order: subject with event type, date, time, mode > salutation > purpose in one sentence > who speaks > agenda in 3 to 4 lines > date, time, venue or link, RSVP deadline > who to contact > sign-off > short disclaimer ("for existing and prospective investors; not an offer"). Offer a recording afterwards (Carnelian archives calls on YouTube).

## Anti-patterns (observed in the peer set, to avoid)
1. Performance only inside an image (Marcellus Jan 2026): not searchable, not readable by assistive tools, and the net-of-fees basis is invisible. Put the sentence and table in text.
2. Stale benchmark data in a current report (Unifi Nov 2025 letter carries a CRISIL table dated 30 Sep 2024). Date every figure and refresh with each issue.
3. A very long disclaimer ahead of the sign-off (Marcellus repeats the sign-off after 550 words). Keep a short disclaimer in the email, full text linked or attached.
4. Claiming rank or "top quartile" without period or source (Buoyant, and AIF-ranking claims in Abakkus decks). Quote only a period, a benchmark and a source.
5. Essay-first updates that put the market view before the client's own result (Buoyant opens with a market essay, performance on page 4). Put the portfolio result first.
6. Exclamation-mark greetings ("Greetings from Team Carnelian!") and emotive thank-you lines clash with the formal tone requested.
