# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Prospective high-net-worth investors with roughly ₹50 lakh to ₹20 crore of investible surplus, existing Moneybee investors, wealth advisors, and compliance-minded family decision makers. They arrive having seen many return-led pitches and are trying to determine whether Moneybee can be trusted with a meaningful portion of family wealth.

## Product Purpose

The public site is Moneybee's first point of institutional due diligence. It must make the distinction between Portfolio Management Services and the Alternative Investment Fund immediately clear, show how risk and performance are evaluated, make the firm's process and regulatory standing inspectable, publish the investment team's reasoning over time, and provide a measured path to a direct conversation.

Success is not a high-pressure conversion. Success is a qualified investor understanding what Moneybee believes, which product they may be eligible for, how to verify the firm's claims, and who they can speak with next.

## Brand Personality

Calm, exacting, established.

Moneybee should feel modern but rooted in tradition: a sharp Indian investment house with enough experience to have stopped shouting. The voice uses conviction rather than enthusiasm, concrete facts rather than abstraction, plain language rather than jargon, and discusses risk willingly.

## Anti-references

- Generic Indian financial-services design, especially navy-and-gold private-bank styling.
- Fintech dashboards, glass cards, glowing gradients, candlesticks, bulls, arrows, and animated counters.
- Generic office meetings, handshakes, skyscraper glass, and stock-photo diversity theatre.
- Aggressive conversion funnels, urgency, sales enthusiasm, and "Invest now" language.
- Luxury-finance templates made from alternating dark sections, oversized serif slogans, and identical card grids.
- Text-wall editorial minimalism where every section is another oversized heading and the page has no visual or interactive life.
- Decorative motion, full-page transitions, and effects that compete with reading.

## Design Principles

1. Show proof before making a claim. Registration, product structure, methodology, people, and published reasoning carry more weight than adjectives.
2. Make the thinking visible. The investment process, risk framework, and research history are the primary content, not decoration around a sales pitch.
3. Reduce words, increase meaning. Each section must introduce a distinct idea or useful interaction; no restated headings or generic brand copy.
4. Give the page a visual life beyond typography. Use diagrams, editorial artefacts, structured data, original materials, and purposeful motion to make ideas inspectable.
5. Preserve the gravity of the decision. Every interaction should feel measured, direct, and suitable for someone evaluating where to place family capital.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG 2.2 AA. Support keyboard navigation, screen readers, visible focus states, adequate touch targets and color contrast. Respect reduced-motion preferences. Do not communicate financial meaning through color alone. Keep disclosure and risk information readable rather than visually buried.
